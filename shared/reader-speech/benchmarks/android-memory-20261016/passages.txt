Along with Richard Trevithick, Evans developed high-pressure steam engines as alternatives to the earlier designs.
Unfortunately, Evans would not live to see his prediction come true. Apprenticed to a wheelwright, he moved in 1791 from his native Delaware to a new town.
In addition to building steam engines, Evans in 1804 provided the Philadelphia Board of Health with a device for dredging and cleaning the city docks, which he called the Oruktor Amphibolos, after the Greek words for an amphibious dredger. His work on a steam wagon did not succeed, and in 1819, worn out by business failures and money troubles, he collapsed after learning that his shop had burned down.
